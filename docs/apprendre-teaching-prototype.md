## A2 — Saying how long something has been happening

### The key rule

If a situation began in the past **and is still true now**, French commonly uses a **present-tense verb + depuis**.

English often uses “have been …” in this situation. Do not automatically copy that English tense into French.

> Je **travaille** dans cette librairie **depuis septembre**.
>
> I **have been working** in this bookshop **since September**.

The work began in September and continues now. **Travaille** is present tense.

A useful noun here: **une librairie (f.)** means a bookshop, not a library. A library is **une bibliothèque (f.)**.

### Two ways to finish the sentence

| What you want to give | Pattern | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| An elapsed duration: how long? | present + depuis + duration | Je travaille ici **depuis six mois**. |
| A starting point: since when? | present + depuis + starting point | Je travaille ici **depuis septembre**. |

The first sentence means “I have been working here for six months.” The second means “I have been working here since September.”

French uses **depuis** in both cases. Do not add an extra **de** before the duration.

### See the rule in different situations

> Nous **attendons** le bus **depuis vingt minutes**.
>
> We **have been waiting** for the bus **for twenty minutes**.

The wait is continuing. French uses **attendons**, not an English-shaped perfect construction.

> Ma sœur **habite** dans ce quartier **depuis 2022**.
>
> My sister **has been living** in this neighbourhood **since 2022**.

The starting point is a year. She still lives there.

> Je **cherche** un appartement **depuis trois semaines**.
>
> I **have been looking** for an apartment **for three weeks**.

The search began three weeks ago and is still in progress.

> **Depuis combien de temps** habitez-vous dans ce quartier ?
>
> **How long have you been living** in this neighbourhood?

Use **depuis combien de temps** to ask for the elapsed duration. **Depuis quand** asks for the starting point.

### Do not confuse “still happening”, “a completed period” and “ago”

| French | English | What the sentence tells us |
| --- | --- | --- |
| J’habite à Québec **depuis deux ans**. | I have been living in Québec City for two years. | I still live there now. |
| J’ai habité à Québec **pendant deux ans**, puis j’ai déménagé. | I lived in Québec City for two years, then I moved. | The period of living there ended. |
| J’ai déménagé à Québec **il y a deux ans**. | I moved to Québec City two years ago. | The move happened two years before now. The sentence alone does not say whether I still live there. |

All three are correct. They communicate different facts. Choose the timeline before choosing the expression.

Here **à Québec** means in Québec City. **Au Québec** refers to the province.

### A useful limitation: depuis does not always require the present

The main rule concerns a **continuing situation**. You can also report completed events within a period:

> J’**ai envoyé** deux messages **depuis lundi**.
>
> I **have sent** two messages **since Monday**.

The two acts of sending are completed. We are counting events since a starting point, not describing one uninterrupted action.

You can also report that an event has not occurred:

> Je **n’ai pas reçu** de réponse **depuis lundi**.
>
> I **have not received** a reply **since Monday**.

This is why “always use the present after depuis” would be a misleading rule. The fuller past-time uses belong in a later lesson.

### Two actual learner errors

**Avoid:** Je travaille ici depuis de six mois.  
**Use:** Je travaille ici depuis six mois.

**Why:** depuis introduces the duration directly; do not insert de.

**Avoid:** Je cherche un appartement depuis que trois semaines.  
**Use:** Je cherche un appartement depuis trois semaines.

**Why:** three weeks is a duration. **Depuis que** introduces a clause containing a subject and a verb, not a duration.

### Use it in a practical exchange

> — Depuis combien de temps cherchez-vous un appartement ?  
> — Je cherche depuis trois semaines. Je préfère ce quartier parce qu’il est proche de mon travail.
>
> — How long have you been looking for an apartment?  
> — I have been looking for three weeks. I prefer this neighbourhood because it is close to my workplace.

The answer gives the requested information, then adds a relevant reason. The grammar supports the message; there is no need to force several advanced structures into this short exchange.

### Separate practice design — not another panel on the lesson page

Assessment would use **new sentences**, not just the examples above:

- Identify whether a situation is still continuing or has ended.
- Choose and form the right tense and time expression without being shown the answer.
- Correct an extra preposition and explain the fix.
- Ask a natural duration question in a different situation.
- Return later to distinguish completed events since a starting point from an ongoing situation.

A learner who only recognises the displayed rule has not yet demonstrated all of these skills.

## B2 specimen — Conceding a point without losing your argument

### Choose the construction before conjugating

| Construction | What follows it | Original example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **bien que** | a clause in the subjunctive in careful standard French | **Bien que le trajet soit plus long**, je préfère prendre le bus. |
| **même si** | a clause in the indicative | **Même si le trajet est plus long**, je préfère prendre le bus. |
| **malgré** | a noun phrase in this construction | **Malgré la durée du trajet**, je préfère prendre le bus. |

Translations: “Although the journey is longer, I prefer taking the bus”; “Even if/even though the journey is longer, I prefer taking the bus”; “Despite the length of the journey, I prefer taking the bus.”

**Soit** is the present subjunctive of **être**; **est** is its present indicative. The different forms come from the constructions.

The subjunctive does **not** automatically mean the information is doubtful or unreal. In the first example, the speaker can accept that the journey really is longer. **Bien que** still calls for the subjunctive in careful standard usage.

**Même si** can introduce an actual concession or a hypothetical one, depending on the context. It does not take the subjunctive merely because the sentence is hypothetical.

### Make the concession useful

> Bien que le trajet **soit** plus long, je préfère prendre le bus : je peux lire pendant le déplacement et je n’ai pas à chercher une place de stationnement.
>
> Although the journey is longer, I prefer taking the bus: I can read during the journey and I do not have to look for a parking space.

The speaker acknowledges a drawback and gives relevant reasons. The sentence is not better simply because it contains a subjunctive; the relationship between the ideas makes it useful.

### Keep an error distinct from a valid alternative

**Avoid in this construction:** Malgré le trajet est plus long, je prends le bus.  
**Use:** Malgré la durée du trajet, je prends le bus.  
**Or:** Bien que le trajet soit plus long, je prends le bus.

**Why:** despite + a noun phrase and although + a clause require different structures. Both corrected versions are valid.

The final B2 lesson would add formation support, further tense-appropriate examples and a separate practice set. This specimen is not a substitute for that complete lesson.
