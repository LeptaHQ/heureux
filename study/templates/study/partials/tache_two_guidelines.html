Task 2: how to practise effectively

Official format: 2 minutes of preparation, then 3 minutes 30 of interaction. Your task is to obtain information in an everyday situation, using the roles given in the prompt.

  • Read the situation first. Identify who needs the information, what is already known, and whether tu or vous fits the relationship. Do not reverse the roles or ask for facts already supplied.
  • Plan topics, not a recital. During preparation, organize the important points in a practical order. These eight-question models are examples, not an official quota or a reason to stop early.
  • Listen and adapt. Let the reply determine your next question. Skip answered points, substitute actual names, dates or places, and use the English follow-up cue only when it fits. If replies are very short, explore a relevant detail or clarify what you genuinely need.
  • Vary naturally. Mix open questions, est-ce que, suitable inversion, polite conditional requests and conversational forms where the register allows. There is no official requirement to use inversion in every question; clarity and responsiveness matter more than forced complexity.
  • Maintain the exchange. Use brief, appropriate reactions and transitions rather than a sequence of disconnected questions. Follow the examiner's timing, and finish courteously with thanks.

FEI assesses vocabulary and grammar, fluency and pronunciation, interaction and coherence, and suitability to the situation. The candidate handbook warns against reciting memorized texts.

NCLC 7/8 is an overall speaking target: IRCC maps 10–11/20 to NCLC 7 and 12–13/20 to NCLC 8. These conversions apply to the complete oral test, not this task alone; no model guarantees a score.

Sources: FEI task format, candidate handbook, September 2026 (pp. 19, 21), IRCC conversions.
