# Expression orale, tâche 3: editorial standard

This standard concerns oral viewpoints only, not oral task 2 or written task 3.
Read every consigne in a semantic group before changing its shared default.
Similar subject matter is not sufficient evidence of equivalence.

## Speaking, not an essay

France Éducation international describes task 3 as a spontaneous, continuous,
convincing response to the examiner's question, **without preparation, lasting
4 minutes 30**. Its assessment considers linguistic control, fluency,
structure/coherence, thematic development and appropriateness to the situation:

- [Official task description](https://www.france-education-international.fr/test/tcf-canada?langue=fr)
- [Official assessment criteria](https://www.france-education-international.fr/article/evaluation-epreuves-tcf?langue=fr)

The six-part teaching structure is an introduction, three developed arguments,
a meaningful nuance and a conclusion. Three arguments and an example for each
are editorial choices, **not official quotas**. There is no official oral word
count. The reviewed models use a 300–500 whitespace-word rehearsal envelope,
not a target length or a guarantee that every text fits 4 minutes 30. A longer
model may need adapting to the learner's pace. Rehearse with pauses; do not rush
to reproduce a text or assume a particular speaking rate. Adapt to the actual
question and examiner's timing. Trim repetition before cutting explanations or
the connections that make an answer understandable.
The language target is NCLC 7 preparation, not a promised score. Prefer familiar
words and connected, expressive phrasing that is easy to say, learn and reuse.

The introduction answers the exact issue and orients the listener without
announcing the whole answer twice. Match the requested perspective: a general
question about parents calls for an opinion about parents, not an unexplained
hypothetical decision as a parent. Keep natural first-person opinions, and use
personal experience or direct advice when the actual question calls for them.
Each argument has a distinct claim, an
explanation of how or why it works, a plausible situated illustration and a
proportionate consequence. Examples must do more than repeat the claim.
The nuance addresses a real condition, limitation or trade-off; the conclusion
synthesizes without a new argument.
Read the response as a complete conversational intervention: each idea should
give the listener a reason to follow the next one. Develop the thought rather
than compressing it into a sequence of short observations. Natural parallel
phrasing and familiar expressions can make the explanation easier to follow and
remember. Use a transition that expresses the actual connection, rather than
relying on numbered points to create coherence.
Name the subject when explaining a condition: "L'amitié suppose qu'on puisse
s'écouter" states what friendship requires, rather than sounding like an
instruction to the listener. Keep direct advice where the question calls for it;
do not mechanically remove every "il faut".

Prefer precise ordinary language, manageable sentences and reusable constructions.
Retain sound wording. Reuse a mechanism only where it answers the prompt:
practical support, emotional continuity, learning through repeated exchanges,
access to opportunities and participation are not interchangeable explanations.
Avoid universal generational judgments, essentialised national identities,
invented studies, unsupported majority claims and implied legal guarantees.
Illustrative situations are possibilities, not reports of the learner's life.

Make the reasoning easy to remember: name who acts, what they do and what changes.
Prefer a direct verb to an abstract label, and a clear answer to stacked negatives.
Simplify the phrasing, not the argument: keep its explanation, concrete example
and necessary limits. A shorter text is not automatically easier to learn, and a
familiar, accurate expression need not be replaced merely for variety.
Introduce examples as part of the spoken explanation, using natural openings
such as "Par exemple" or "Prenons l'exemple" where they help. Connect the
situation to its significance instead of listing successive actions. Keep
details that make the mechanism clear, not extra logistics that turn an ordinary
example into a procedural case study. Do not impose identical openings or a
connector quota on every paragraph.
Make necessary premises explicit before using them: someone needs a job to take
leave from work, and a disagreement between relatives needs a clear distinction
between their workplace and a family gathering. Name what a condition concerns,
rather than leaving "say no" or "the next explanation" without a clear referent.
A fictional example may state concrete actions. Do not resolve an uncertain
situation merely by assuming that a helper is available or a request succeeds. Separate
needing time, being able to take leave and being able to afford a journey.
Check these points in the French itself; a clearer English gloss must not hide a
missing premise in the spoken model.
Do not make an example depend on technical food names or abstract labels.
Explain the same cause and result in ordinary words; for example, "garder le
poisson pendant les voyages en mer" rather than "histoire maritime", and "une
façon de cuisiner du pays" rather than "savoir-faire culinaire local".

## Independent human-style GPT review

For every substantive response revision, the writing agent must obtain a critique
from a separate GPT agent before declaring the work complete. Self-review and
passing automated checks are not substitutes for this independent reading.
Give the critic the exact consigne, every equivalent variant, the full current
intervention and the preserved original. Ask it to read as a human listener,
not merely check the six headings or approve grammatical sentences.

The critic must examine whether the position answers the question, the three
arguments are distinct and worthwhile, each explanation leads naturally into a
realistic example, and the result actually follows. Check clear referents,
cause and effect, necessary limits, accurate claims, connected spoken delivery
and familiar NCLC 7 language. Retain strong existing reasoning and reuse
applicable words, phrases, expressions and examples; do not force the same
answer onto different questions or paraphrase sound wording just for variety.

Begin the content audit with each complete English intervention against every
consigne, rather than reviewing isolated passages. Use this reading to expose
missing explanations, overlapping arguments and weak examples. Then independently
check the complete French: it must carry the reasoning in idiomatic spoken
language, not depend on a clearer English gloss. Read it aloud as a connected
answer. Check the full English translation against the final French too: a
revised French passage must not keep an outdated English gloss, and updating
the English Pistes alone does not update the full response.

Resolve substantive findings, reread the complete intervention and obtain a
follow-up critique of the revised answer within the same review scope. Keep
review evidence in session artifacts, not repository planning files. Report real
remaining uncertainty; do not claim literal perfection or loop on cosmetic
synonyms. Preserve protected models, source identities and learning data.

## Argument substance and comparative review

Compare the current model with the preserved original before revising it. Keep
or recover a stronger sound original reason when an edit has reduced it to a
minor convenience. For example, following a sustained line of thought develops
reflection and concentration; merely being able to pause a book is not a
substitute for that intellectual benefit. Reflection and sustained attention
belong to one mechanism, not two nominally different arguments.

Check the job of each paragraph: the claim states what supports the position;
the development explains why or how in a short, clear causal sequence; the
example demonstrates that mechanism in a recognizable situation; the consequence
states what follows. Do not repeat the same assertion in four forms. An example
needs a person or group, a specific action or event, and a relevant result.
Adding "Par exemple" to a general assertion does not make it an illustration.
An example that fits all three arguments unchanged is too generic.

When comparing generations, keep the same criterion: disagreement is not
necessarily disrespect. A concern about school safety is not by itself a
judgment about young people. Connect the illustration to the actual group and
comparison in the claim. Repeated images can distort perceived frequency, but
do not establish how common a behaviour is.

State the claim at the level of the argument, rather than listing its examples.
Financial difficulties can be the claim; irregular income, housing costs and
daily expenses belong in its explanation, followed by a situated example.
General does not mean vague: name the benefit or risk and explain its cause.
Do not replace a substantive benefit with a small feature of the example.
The consequence states what follows from the support, rather than introducing
fresh advice about how to avoid the problem.

The three paragraphs should make distinct contributions to the exact position,
rather than list three side effects of one benefit. Preserve necessary
qualifications without turning a clear argument into empty caution. Favor clear
referents, ordinary precise vocabulary and manageable spoken sentences; literate French need not be
abstract or difficult to decode. Reuse useful language across suitable questions
while removing redundant reasoning within the same intervention. Reread the
whole intervention after assembling edits, not only its individual fields.

Distinguish speaking a language from knowing shared cultural references.
Someone can understand the words without recognizing the reference behind a joke.
Explain that distinction in ordinary language rather than forcing an abstract
label into the introduction.

Read a conversational model as one connected intervention, not fifteen separate
paragraphs. Each explanation should lead naturally into its example and result.
Use transitions that express a real connection; adding fillers or replacing every
subject with "on" is not a substitute for that connection.
For subtitles, connect the steps explicitly: we see an action but cannot
understand the spoken explanation; subtitles in a language we know translate
that explanation; we can then understand the reason. Do not assume that images
alone explain a motive, or that subtitles in an unfamiliar language solve the gap.

Work and skills are parts of one economic contribution, not opposing categories.
An answer can recognize that contribution alongside cultural and civic benefits
without making a person's dignity depend on economic usefulness.

### English response glosses

The full EO3 response can be viewed in French or English. French is the default;
the global choice is remembered, while argument-number and subsection-heading
overrides are temporary. The number switches the whole argument. A heading
switches only its introduction, development, example, consequence, nuance or
conclusion block. Practice retains the bilingual Pistes-first layout.

English is prepared once with DeepL, not requested on visits or clicks:
`python manage.py prepare_eo3_translations --dry-run` reports the exact pending
character count. Prepare explicitly with `--prepare --confirm-characters COUNT`,
then review the English and run `--check`. Preparation is allowance-checked,
atomic and resumable without automatic billable retries. Changed French requires
a fresh exact-text translation; existing records are retained.

Only public spoken model text and public context are sent. Personal edits are
never uploaded; a private block gets English only when its text matches a cached
public block exactly. Keep the canonical French DOM, annotation offsets, saved
notes, French audio and response-copy text unchanged. English glosses stay
excluded from the canonical French annotation stream. Their Highlight action
uses separate, revision-scoped English anchors; French notes and translated
notes must never use those anchors. Controls remain outside annotation/selection
tools. Retain the provider original and a review note whenever an English
post-edit is needed. A revised public block can also receive an editorial
translation without a provider call; label it accurately rather than inventing
a DeepL original. When correcting an existing editorial translation, retain its
complete preceding row in `editorial_history` and explain the correction in the
current `review_note`.

Benefits do not establish necessity. When a consigne says "indispensable", a
balanced answer can develop two substantial benefits and a supported reason why
they are not a universal requirement. Do not force a weak third benefit merely
to keep all three paragraphs positive. A counterexample must actually lack the
relationship or resource under discussion: having few local friends does not
demonstrate living without local relationships. A small practical example can be
clear without being strong enough to carry a broad claim about wellbeing.

apply; otherwise reuse only the compatible passage or concrete case. Do not invent

### Annotation field identities and publication staging

The detail and practice-back templates mark each reviewed model field without
changing its text or whitespace. Private annotation `response_anchor` metadata
contains only the field identifier, SHA-256 checksum and relative UTF-16 start/end.
When the entire field is unchanged, its checksum and exact selected text prove
placement even if both neighbours were rewritten. If the field changed, the
conservative exact-quote/context matcher stays confined to that field. Missing,
retired or ambiguous fields leave the selection stored, not guessed elsewhere.
Cross-field selections and unscoped legacy rows retain the conservative matcher.

Argument identities default to `[1, 2, 3]` for existing models. An optional
`argument_ids` list assigns three distinct positive integers independent of spoken
order. Keep an existing argument's ID through a targeted revision or intentional
reordering; assign a fresh, never-recycled ID to a genuinely new reason. Retiring
an argument does not authorize its quote to move into another argument. These IDs
are presentation metadata, outside response bodies, legacy digests and source
signatures. Do not infer continuity automatically from matching examples.

For the initial rollout, publish the capture runtime and compatible anchor schema
**without editorial changes**, and verify that this version is serving requests.
The column needs a persistent database default (`{}`), not just Python's `dict`
default, so preceding workers can still insert notes/highlights while migrations
run. Migration 0059 repairs already-applied 0058 databases; the replacement
0058/0059 migration adds the column with that default in one operation on fresh
upgrades. Keep both original migrations for partially upgraded databases.
Then run
`python manage.py backfill_response_anchors` against the same still-published
catalogue after the old workers have drained. A separate preservation-only build
can run that command through `deploy_database`; no model text may change in it.
This second pass closes the window where the original workers could save an

locks only each annotation row, and updates only verified anchor metadata, not

Unverifiable older rows remain unscoped and are reported explicitly.

Only after capture and the post-cutover backfill are confirmed may an editorial

## Famille learning consistency

The 17 Famille consignes use 14 Famille models plus the shared Culture model
about friendship and happiness. Keep the truth-with-children and money-for-grades

friends. Likewise, feeling comfortable at one's parents' home is a legitimate
reason in its own right, not only when it finances a plan to move out. The
after-25 prompt asks about practicality, not a mandatory age of independence.

Each argument needs its own mechanism, a plausible action and an observable
result. Distinguish shared expenses from help during temporary incapacity,
privacy online from time limits, and family affection from freedom to confide.
For rewards, illustrate what happens when the same effort no longer earns money;
do not infer dependence simply from paying for one school subject but not another.
Honest feedback must actually name the difficulty and offer a way forward.
Working with a relative requires evidence of relevant collaboration, not an
assumption that knowing the person privately proves professional competence.
Show favoritism in the selection process rather than treating a colleague's
greater seniority as proof that appointing a relative was unfair.

Distance can involve missing ordinary family moments, lacking nearby practical

to practise the local language without assuming the parents already speak it.

A nuance should answer a real limitation of the position, not collect unrelated

whether friends themselves are necessary.
Other affection, personal purpose and regular social participation can matter
without making an acquaintance equivalent to a close confidant.
For happiness, retain the stronger sequence of friendship's emotional support,
affection through other relationships and satisfaction in a personal activity.
Connect the concession to the alternatives explicitly; do not merge the two
alternatives and replace them with further benefits of friendship.
